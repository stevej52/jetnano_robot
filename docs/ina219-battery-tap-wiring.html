INA219 Battery Tap Wiring

Four wires to the Jetson header, one to the battery. Pins 17, 27, 28 and 30 on the Orin's 40-pin header, all in one corner. The board sits on /dev/i2c-1 at address 0x41, voltage only. This is the wiring recorded when it was fitted on 2026-09-30.

The five wires

INA219 pinGoes toWhy
VCCJetson pin 17 (3.3 V)Logic power. Pin 17 is the 3.3 V nearest the I2C pins; pin 1 works too.
GNDJetson pin 30 (GND)Right next to pin 28. Any header GND is fine (25, 20, 34, 39).
SCLJetson pin 28I2C bus 1 clock.
SDAJetson pin 27I2C bus 1 data. Pins 27/28 are /dev/i2c-1 on the Orin (they were i2c-0 on the old Nano).
Vin−Battery +, on the battery side of the power jackThe chip reads the voltage on Vin− against GND. Nothing goes to Vin+: the on-board 0.1 Ω shunt is rated about 3 A and a motor stall is ten times that.

A0 jumper bridged. The default address 0x40 is taken: bus 1 already has the module's own power monitor at 0x40, and bus 7 has the PCA9685 at 0x40. Bridged A0 puts the INA219 at 0x41, which is what battery_monitor expects.

Not bus 7. Pins 3/5 carry the servo board and the BNO055. The INA219 goes on the spare bus so a wobble on it cannot upset steering.

Check it after plugging in

# the board answers at 0x41 on bus 1 (0x40 is the module's own monitor, leave it)
sudo i2cdetect -y -r 1

# the pack voltage, once the robot software is up
ros2 topic echo /battery --once
  • Expect the pack voltage (about 12.4 V on a fresh 3S pack). Under 6 V reads as "no battery", which is what you see when she is on the bench supply.
  • If 0x41 is missing, check VCC first: a board on 3.3 V with its pull-ups on is the usual reason a bus goes quiet.
  • The battery negative and the Jetson ground share the same rail, so the single GND wire to pin 30 is the voltage reference too.
