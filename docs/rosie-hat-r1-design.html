Rosie Hat R1

One board for the robot's electronics, plugged into the stock NVIDIA dev-kit carrier. Design requirements and architecture for layout, drawn up 2026-10-01 for Steve Johnson. It reuses the robot circuits already checked in the RosiePCB R1 schematic (2026-09-30) and leaves the NVIDIA carrier alone: no high-speed routing, no NVIDIA firmware images, no custom EEPROM or BSP.

Why a hat and not the full carrier. Everything Rosie needs from a new board (16+ servo/ESC channels with power cutoffs, four current/voltage sensors, the upgraded IMU, lights, cliff sensors, CAN for a motor controller) lives on a 4-layer board with nothing faster than 10 MHz SPI. The expensive, one-shot part of the full carrier is re-creating NVIDIA's half. A hat costs about one-tenth, can be revised, and when it is proven on the robot it is the robot section of a future carrier.

1. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Replace every loose module and jumper on Rosie: the PCA9685 breakout, the INA219, the BNO055, the relay on pin 7, the cliff-sensor plan, the light switches.
  • 32 servo/ESC channels (16 required available after Rosie's five), with power cutoffs per bank, a hardware watchdog latch and a physical E-STOP loop.
  • Four current/voltage sensors with state-of-charge on the pack.
  • BNO086 IMU (upgraded from BNO055).
  • CAN bus for an ODrive-class motor controller.
  • Keep the dev kit's 40-pin header fully usable: the hat shares its buses and uses a handful of GPIO, documented pin by pin.
  • Safe to build in small quantity: standard parts, hand-repairable packages where possible, JLCPCB/PCBWay assembly.

Non-goals (deliberately)

  • No regulators on the board. The Jetson's header supplies the small stuff: its 3.3 V (pins 1/17, fused on the hat, budget ≤ 250 mA) runs all the hat's logic, the IMU, the CAN chip and the four cliff sensors; its 5 V (pins 2/4, budget ≤ 500 mA) is passed through and available for small 5 V loads. Everything bigger arrives from one of Steve's separate BECs on its own connector: the four servo banks (J140–J143), the 5 V / 3 A lights (J130) and the 12 V contactor coil (J152). The hat switches, fuses, senses and distributes; it never converts. The only on-board regulation is the IMU's local LDO carried over from R1's sheet.
  • No traction current on the board. The ESC's 30 A stays in the chassis harness; the hat carries sense leads and drives an external contactor.
  • No Jetson power on the board. The dev kit keeps its 9–20 V jack. (A separate small power board for the charge jack / ORing stays on the list.)
  • No RTC: the dev kit has one and its battery is fitted.
  • No USB devices: lidar, camera, microphone stay on the carrier's USB.
  • No firmware MCU in R1. Pulse generation is the PCA9685; the safety logic is hardware (watchdog + latch), as in RosiePCB R1. An RP2040 "neutral-on-fault" option is noted for R2.

3. Mechanical

  • Form: a separate 100 × 80 mm board, 4 layers, mounted in the case where the harness runs, joined to the dev kit by a 40-way IDC ribbon (≤ 150 mm) from J12. Not stacked on the header: the heatsink and fan sit beside J12 and the case is tight. tbd confirm position, and whether a shrouded (keyed) header is wanted at both ends — recommended, so it cannot go on backwards.
  • M3 holes at the corners, 3.2 mm; all connectors on the two long edges; servo headers in four labelled banks on one edge; power and sense on the other.
  • Keep the IMU away from the bank switches and the CAN transceiver, on the side of the board nearest the chassis centre; record its installed axes on the silkscreen (the software transform is read from there).
  • Silkscreen every connector's pin 1, voltage and the words signal only on the ESC header.

4. The 40-pin header contract

Every pin keeps its stock function. The hat shares the two I2C buses (pull-ups once, on the hat, 2.2 kΩ to 3.3 V, with the dev kit's own pull-ups considered) and takes SPI0 and four GPIO. The ribbon carries all 40; unused pins are simply passed through to a second header on the hat for future use.

6. Servo / ESC outputs and the safety chain

Thirty-two 3-pin headers in four banks. Unlike RosiePCB R1 (signal only), the hat carries servo power through a switched bank, so a cutoff cuts power, not just the pulse. The ESC is the exception: its header is signal-only and its power runs through an external contactor the hat drives.

  • Watchdog + latch: RosiePCB R1's circuit as drawn (TPS3431 window watchdog on HEARTBEAT, latch set by an ARM rising edge, cleared by timeout ≈ 225 ms, ARM low, brownout, or E-STOP). It drives both PCA9685 OE pins and every bank enable. Re-arm needs a fresh ARM edge. R1
  • E-STOP loop: a 2-pin connector for a normally-closed external mushroom switch; open = latch clear. Shunt fitted when no switch is wired. new
  • Overcurrent: the traction INA228's ALERT pin into the latch clear, threshold set in software (and a hardware default strap). new
  • What a cutoff does to the ESC: loses its pulse and loses power at the contactor. The handoff's warning stands: prove on the bench that the real ESC stops, and keep the physical kill switch.
  • Startup: all banks off, ARM low, lights off, PCA9685 outputs tri-state until the software sets safe pulses and arms. Status LEDs: ARMED, LATCH CLEAR, per-bank ON/FAULT.

7. Current and voltage sensing

Four INA228 (85 V, 20-bit, charge and energy accumulators) on bus 1. All four shunts are off the board, in the chassis harness, with 4-wire Kelvin leads to a JST-PH 4-pin per channel (V+ sense, shunt+, shunt−, GND). That keeps 30 A off a 100 × 80 mm board and lets the shunt sit where the current actually flows.

8. IMU, cliff sensors, lights, CAN

  • BNO086 on SPI0 (CS0 = header pin 24), mode 3, ≤ 3 MHz, exactly as R1's checked sheet — reused rather than redrawn for I2C. INT/WAKE/RESET on header pins 15/32/33. VDDIO at 3.3 V (the header's SPI level; the part is rated 1.65–3.6 V). Local LDO and 32.768 kHz crystal per the R1 sheet; keep it rigid and away from heat and switch currents. R1
  • Cliff sensors: R1's four fused 3.3 V branches behind the TCA9548A at 0x73 (J120–J123: GND, fused 3.3 V, SDA, SCL), ESD on the signals, ≤ 40 mA each, for VL53L0X breakouts. R1
  • Lights: R1's two 5 V / 3 A high-side switches with fault reporting, external 5 V in on J130, outputs J131/J132, independent of the motion inhibit, off at power-up. R1
  • CAN: MCP2517FD (SOIC-14) on SPI0 CS1 (20 MHz crystal, INT on GPIO11) with a 3.3 V transceiver (SN65HVD230-class), two 4-pin CAN connectors (CAN_H, CAN_L, GND, 12 V pass-through optional) for daisy-chaining, 120 Ω termination on a jumper. Appears to Linux as can0 through the in-kernel driver; no firmware. The ODrive is a separate unit on its own power. new

12. Cost and risk

  • Schematic status (2026-10-01): the R1 KiCad project exists — 12 sheets, ERC 0 violations, 274 parts, BOM exported; see HAT-NOTES.txt in the package (C:\jetson\RosieHat osie-hat-r1.zip, H2-Host ~/rosie-hat).
  • Bare boards: five 4-layer 100 × 80 mm, roughly $40–60 at JLCPCB (calculator figure, not a quote).
  • Assembly of two boards plus parts: roughly $250–450 depending on the INA228/BNO086 stock and the smart switches. Nothing on the board needs a firmware image or a licence.
  • A mistake costs one revision of this board, not the Jetson: the dev kit is never flashed, modified or powered from the hat. The 3.3 V the hat takes from the header is fused on the hat.
  • Bring-up order: power the hat from a bench supply with the ribbon disconnected; check rails; connect the ribbon with every bank supply off; scan both I2C buses (expect 0x20, 0x40, 0x42, 0x73 on bus 7 and 0x41–0x44, 0x4A on bus 1); prove ARM/heartbeat/latch with a scope and no loads; then one bank at a time with real servos; the ESC last, with the contactor.
